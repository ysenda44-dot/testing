# International (non-Japanese) agencies and boutique studios for luxury, department store, mall, airport and travel-retail work: a reference directory for a multi-brand airport luxury-boutique LP

> How these notes were gathered: about 40 web searches, after which the shared web-search budget for this turn ran out. WebFetch was blocked by the session's network allowlist for tympanus.net (Codrops), immersive-g.com and 14islands.com, so some details below come from search-result snippets and summaries rather than full page reads. Where only one source supports a claim, the entry says so. Studios named in the brief that I could not verify are listed under the "Gaps" of each section, with nothing invented.
> Type labels used: [GLOBAL] = global brand or design agency; [DIGITAL] = digital or web studio; [LUXURY] = luxury creative agency; [BOUTIQUE] = independent boutique; [SPACE] = retail, interior or wayfinding firm; [AD] = advertising agency.

## Key Question 1: Which global brand and design agencies have airport and luxury-retail work? Who branded or designed Heathrow retail, Jewel Changi, Changi T4, Hamad International, Extime (Paris Aéroport), DFS/T Galleria, La Samaritaine, Selfridges, Harrods and K11?

### Takeaway
Most famous airport and luxury-retail destinations were not branded by the big "brand agency" names you might expect. Their identities come from specialists or from networked ad agencies: Base Design (JFK T4), Tribal Worldwide (Jewel), Start Design (Hamad), Be-pôles (La Samaritaine logo), W Conran Design (Extime communications), Dynamo (Dublin/Cork duty free) and St Luke's (Heathrow retail campaigns). The physical environments come from spatial firms: Benoy, Chapman Taylor, SAA, Safdie and Yabu Pushelberg. Among the big names, Pentagram has the strongest verified luxury department-store portfolio: Liberty, Saks, Selfridges x Alexander McQueen, Mytheresa and Landmark (Hong Kong). The clearest documented commercial result for an airport luxury brand is Extime Paris. In 2024 its spend per passenger reached €87.60 in the renovated CDG T1 international area, and Extime Duty Free Paris sales rose 8.3% to €819m.

### Inferences
- Airport retail brands are usually built by small specialist teams (Base, Dynamo, Start, W Conran, Be-pôles), not by the most famous global agencies. For an airport LP, the best visual references are therefore the destination sites themselves (extime.com, JFK T4's digital presence, Changi and Jewel) plus luxury-maison web work (Question 3), rather than global-agency case studies.
- Extime is the best business-case anchor. Its renovated T1 international area reached €87.60 spend per passenger against an average of €32.10. That supports pitching a "boutique terminal" narrative of curated, gallery-like presentation and hospitality cues, rather than a duty-free-supermarket tone.
- Several operators (DFS, Avolta) have moved toward consolidated master brands. A multi-brand LP should lead with the airport's own curatorial voice and treat the maison logos as a grid or "gallery wall", not let each brand's identity dominate.

The web-search budget ran out before those searches. Landor's own travel page lists City of Melbourne and Oman but no airports ([Landor & Fitch](https://emea.landorandfitch.com/travelanddestination)).
- No agency credit was found for the T Galleria identity (2013), the Avolta identity (an unnamed UK agency), the K11 MUSEA identity, or Selfridges' and Harrods' current (2018–2026) digital or identity work.
- Search summaries mentioned Pentagram wayfinding for Toronto Pearson T1, but I did not capture the exact project URL, so this is unverified.
- No case study reported quantified results (sales, footfall, conversion) for any of the airport campaigns (Heathrow, Jewel, JFK T4). Extime's figures are operator results, not agency-attributed ones.

## Key Question 2: Luxury-specialist creative agencies: notable luxury clients and projects

### Takeaway
Of the luxury-specialist names in the brief, I could verify these: Baron & Baron (the 2019 Zara logo; Fabien Baron's creative direction for Dior and Maison Margiela), M/M (Paris) (Balenciaga, Louis Vuitton, Loewe, Prada), Commission Studio (Fenty; clients including LVMH, Tiffany, Dior Parfums and TAG Heuer), Wednesday Agency (Calvin Klein, Net-a-Porter, Alexander McQueen, Galeries Lafayette; majority-owned by BBDO since 2016) and Kurppa Hosk (O.A. ### Inferences
- For an airport luxury LP, the useful lessons from these studios are typographic. Baron & Baron and Saville show restrained high-contrast serifs or ultra-clean sans with generous white space. Commission's luxury print sensibility points toward a monochrome canvas that lets each maison's imagery carry the colour.
- Since most of the brands on the user's airport floor (Dior, Burberry, Zara group, LVMH maisons) already use these studios' typographic codes, a neutral, gallery-like LP frame avoids clashing with maison logos.

### Gaps
- I could not verify portfolios for Bureau Borsche, Zak Group, OK-RM, Spin, Studio Frith, Order, Partners & Spade, Lundgren+Lindqvist, Bielke&Yang or &Walsh. Searches for them were not run because the web-search budget was exhausted.
- I found no source confirming Wednesday Agency work for Selfridges or Mulberry, nor any luxury retail identity by Mucho.
- None of these studios published quantified business results in the sources found.

### Inferences
- Cartier is the brand most consistently used as a showcase by award-winning boutiques (Immersive Garden, 14islands, Merci-Michel, Bonhomme). Its Watches & Wonders and "365" sites are the best web references for a premium, narrative scroll with product hero moments. These patterns transfer well to a "maison of the month" or "featured boutique" section on an airport LP.
- AKQA's Loewe "art gallery-inspired" storefront (+165% sales, +108% conversion, vendor-reported) is the clearest evidence that a gallery-like, minimal multi-product grid can lift commercial results. It is a relevant pattern for a directory of many boutiques.
- WebGL-heavy studios (Lusion, Resn, Active Theory) are good for a hero moment, but an airport LP must load fast on mobile in terminals. The safer approach is a lightweight hero with Cartier-style restraint rather than a full 3D experience.

## Key Question 5 (from the objective): Which projects are best to study for a multi-brand airport luxury-boutique LP?

### Takeaway
Ranked by relevance:
1. **Extime Paris** (extime.com, CDG T1 "flagship boutique terminal"): a live multi-brand airport luxury and hospitality site, with documented commercial uplift of €87.60 spend per passenger in renovated T1.
2. **JFK T4 by Base Design**: an airport identity carried consistently into digital, with a custom typeface and a "Guide & Delight" tone.
3. **Heathrow T2 and T5 luxury** (Chapman Taylor environment; St Luke's "Paradise" and "Be Spoilt for Choice" campaigns; Avolta "Redefine Your Beauty" multi-brand activation).
4. **Jewel and Changi T4** (Tribal Worldwide, Benoy).
5. **Maison-level web craft**: Cartier "365" (14islands), Cartier Watches & Wonders (Immersive Garden), The Fabulous Cartier Journey (Merci-Michel), Louis Vuitton "Maison of All Victories" (Cosmic Shelter), "The Maison Dior" (AKQA) and dior.com (Mazarine).
6. **Loewe e-commerce (AKQA)**: a gallery-style storefront with vendor-reported sales and conversion uplift.
7. **Pentagram's department-store identities** (Liberty, Saks, Landmark) for typographic systems.

### Inferences
Suggested LP pattern library, synthesised from the cited references:
1. **Hero**: the airport's own curatorial voice, in the manner of Extime's "Extra Time" hospitality framing, not a single brand.
2. **"Boutique avenue"**: a horizontal maison scroller, mirroring the Chapman Taylor and Hyphen walk-through layouts.
3. **Featured-maison story module**: the Cartier and Dior narrative-scroll craft.
4. **Gallery grid of all boutiques with filters**: the AKQA Loewe "art gallery" storefront logic.
5. **Wayfinding and terminal map**: Benoy T4, and the Base JFK T4 typographic system.
6. **Services**: reserve-and-collect, beauty activations and gift-with-purchase offers, as in Heathrow and Avolta.

For agency shortlisting if the user later commissions work: Immersive Garden, 14islands, Bonhomme and Merci-Michel for luxury web craft; Base Design and Dynamo for airport identity systems; AKQA and Mazarine for luxury e-commerce at scale.

### Gaps
- None of the airport LPs (extime.com, Heathrow, Changi) has a public agency credit or published web-performance metrics in the sources found. Their web-build agencies remain unknown.
- Business results for the boutique web studios' luxury projects (Cartier, LV, Dior) were not published in the sources found. Only AKQA's Loewe figures (vendor-hosted) and Extime's operator figures are quantified.

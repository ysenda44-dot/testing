# Multi-brand / multi-house directory sites: references for a "all luxury boutiques of a venue" landing page (non-airport, 2019–2026)

Research method note for the writer. I ran 36 web searches. WebFetch failed with "getaddrinfo ENOTFOUND www.lvmh.com": the host is outside the session's allowed network, so it has to be allowed before any page can be fetched. I made no further fetches. As a result:
- **Cited Findings** hold only facts that appeared in search results, each with its source.
- **Inferences** hold two things. The first is presentation-pattern descriptions marked **[PK]** ("prior knowledge, not verified in this session; confirm when screenshotting"). The second is "what to borrow" recommendations.
- Each **Image URL** is labeled as either (seen) or (constructed, verify). "(seen)" means the exact URL appeared in search results. "(constructed, verify)" means it is my best guess at the brand-listing page and must be opened and checked before screenshotting.

Overall headline: award-winning sites whose main job is listing many brands are **rare**.
- In 2025–2026, Awwwards awards in the luxury category went mostly to **single-brand** campaign or microsites (Cartier, Miu Miu, Maison Alaïa, The Maison Dior). They did not go to group directories, multi-brand retailers or district sites.
- The best-documented multi-property *results* come from **hospitality collections** (Aman, Small Luxury Hotels) and multi-brand **e-commerce** (Mytheresa via Monetate). Those results are vendor-reported.
- The best-documented *agency credits* for multi-exhibitor platforms are Pentagram (Frieze), Linked by Air (TEFAF Online), Jakala (Salone del Mobile), AQ (Tokyo Art Beat), Zee Group (Comité Colbert) and Inviqa / Nucleus / Matter of Form (Aman).

None of the group "our maisons" sites turned up a verifiable web award or a public agency credit. The exception is Comité Colbert, whose site redesign was won by **Zee Group** after a competitive pitch.

### Inferences
- The award gap is structural. Group directories are investor- and talent-facing and conservative, while web awards reward immersive single-brand storytelling. For a "boutiques of a venue" page, the best approach is to take the **information architecture from group directories**, the **visual treatment from single-brand SOTD sites**, and the **filter and index mechanics from multi-brand e-commerce** (Key Question 2).
- [PK] LVMH Our Maisons groups maisons by the six sectors with a sector filter or tabs. Each maison is shown as an image-led card (campaign or architecture photo plus the maison name), linking to a maison detail page. Expect a restrained serif/sans pairing, a white or ivory ground and photography-heavy cards. **Borrow:** a category tab row (e.g. Fashion / Jewellery & Watches / Beauty / Dining) above an image-card grid. A sector count badge ("75 Maisons") gives instant scale.
- [PK] Kering "Houses" uses large full-bleed image tiles per house with the house wordmark, in an editorial, magazine-like rhythm. That fits the cited "magazine styling" description. **Borrow:** treat each boutique as an editorial "story tile" rather than a logo cell, which suits a smaller set of 10–30 boutiques.
- Comité Colbert is the closest institutional analogue: an association presenting about 98 independent maisons under one graphic signature. **Borrow:** a unifying frame device (their "Carré Colbert" square) applied to every member's tile so that disparate brand imagery reads as one family.
- Richemont's W&W 2026 group page is a clean model of "a group presents its maisons at one venue/event". It is directly analogous to "a venue presents its boutiques".

- No search results for the Prada Group, Capri Holdings, Tapestry, Puig, OTB, Swatch Group, Fondazione Altagamma or Walpole websites, whether for agency, awards or redesign results. I did not search these individually because of the search budget.
- I could not identify the "Japanese luxury brand association (日本の高級ブランド団体)" from the brief. No organisation surfaced, so it needs the coordinator to clarify the name.

### Inferences
- [PK] Multi-brand e-commerce designer pages share one near-universal pattern:
  - A text-only **A–Z designer index** with a sticky letter jump bar (A B C … #), a "search designers" type-ahead and a "Featured / New designers" strip above.
  - SSENSE is the purest version: a monochrome Helvetica-style list, no imagery and very dense.
  - Net-a-Porter, MR PORTER, Mytheresa, Farfetch and 24S add hero imagery or curated "edits" above the index.
  - DSM's e-shop is deliberately raw and brand-list-first, true to the CdG/Kawakubo aesthetic.
  - **Borrow:** for a venue LP, put a compact A–Z index with a letter bar and type-ahead *below* an image-led "featured boutiques" carousel. The index serves the shopper who already knows the brand. The imagery serves discovery.
- [PK] Japanese department store and mall sites (GINZA SIX, 三越伊勢丹) generally organise shop lists by **floor + category + 50音/ABC**, with a floor-map toggle. **Borrow:** for a physical venue, the floor/zone dimension is a filter that pure e-commerce lacks and that venue visitors need.
- The Mytheresa results suggest that **personalisation of what is shown first** moves conversion more than visual redesign. A venue LP could reorder featured boutiques by visitor language/nationality, which is an inference from the Mytheresa China conversion lift.

### Inferences
- **[PK] Presentation patterns to confirm on screenshot:**
  - **NAP / MR PORTER:** a "Designers" page with an A–Z list, sticky letter jump bar and search box. A "featured designers" row sits above; editorial content (PORTER / The Journal) lives in separate hubs. Monochrome sans UI with large editorial photography on the homepage.
  - **Farfetch:** a designer A–Z plus a unique **"Boutiques" directory** of partner stores (each boutique with city and story). This is the closest e-commerce analogue to "many boutiques in one place".
  - **LVR:** a dense black-and-white designer list.
  - **Browns:** a designer list wrapped in the Spring identity.
  - **Bergdorf:** a designer A–Z inside a lavender/white brand palette, with editorial "5th & 58th".
  - **Neiman / Harrods / Selfridges:** department-store "Designers / Brands A–Z" indexes. Harrods uses its green and gold; Selfridges its yellow.
  - **The Webster:** a pastel, Miami Art Deco tone.
  - **10 Corso Como:** Kris Ruhs circle graphics.
- **Borrow:**
  1. **Farfetch's typographic system** (custom sans plus editorial serif, monogram) shows how one quiet frame can hold hundreds of louder brand logos.
  2. **Farfetch's "Boutiques" directory** gives each store a card with place and story, which is exactly the venue-LP unit.
  3. **Bergdorf's 5th & 58th and window archive** show that an editorial layer and a "visual memory" of the physical store raise a directory above a list.
  4. **Speed is a design requirement.** Farfetch's −1.3% conversion per +100 ms LCP argues for lazy-loaded image grids and lightweight logo assets on a many-brand LP.
  5. **The MATCHES lesson** is about business, not design: a strong editorial site did not prevent collapse. The venue LP should tie each boutique card to an action (visit, reserve, shop).
- Ownership changes ([PK], unverified here): YNAP (NAP, MR PORTER) moved from Richemont to Mytheresa/LuxExperience in 2025, and Farfetch was acquired by Coupang in early 2024. Check before quoting owners.

## Key Question 2: Korea / China online luxury (The Hyundai, Shinsegae SSG, Tmall Luxury Pavilion, JD Luxury)

### Takeaway
These Asian platforms present luxury as **brand "halls" (전문관 / Maison stores) inside a larger mall**. That is a useful model for a venue LP giving each boutique its own micro-storefront.
- The best-documented result: SSG LUXURY ordering members **+27% YoY** (Mar 2024).
- The newest launch: **The Hyundai Hi (더현대 하이)**, opened 6 Apr 2026 with about 3,000 brands and brand halls for Max Mara, Maison Margiela and RRL.
- No agency or award credits were found.

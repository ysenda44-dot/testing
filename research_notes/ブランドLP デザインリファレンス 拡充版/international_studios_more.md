## Key Question 1: The studios left unverified last time. What luxury, fashion, beauty or retail work can now be verified?

### Takeaway
Of the 35 studios left unverified in the first pass, these now have solid luxury or retail credits:
- **Work & Co**: LOEWE 2025, JW Anderson 2025, Givenchy 2022, Céline 2017, Phoebe Philo.
- **Bureau Borsche**: the Balenciaga logotype; the Rimowa wordmark, Jan 2018.
- **Zak Group**: a Prada invite in the D&AD Annual 2022; the Paco Rabanne identity.

- **Checkland Kindleysides**: Johnstons of Elgin London flagship; Hogan, Via Montenapoleone.

Work & Co is the strongest single find for a luxury LP. It is the web and e-commerce partner behind several LVMH and Kering-adjacent maisons. Wolff Olins, Collins, Landor and Koto showed no luxury work in the sources found. Media.Monks, UNIT9, Active Theory and ManvsMachine returned nothing relevant. Eleven studios could not be searched before the budget ran out (see Gaps).

### Inferences
- **Work & Co** is the most relevant "famous agency" for a luxury multi-brand LP. It has repeatedly delivered e-commerce for LVMH- and Richemont-era maisons and "Phoebe Philo"-grade minimalism. Its LOEWE (2025) and JW Anderson (2025) pages are the best current references for a quiet luxury grid-plus-editorial layout.
- **Bureau Borsche** and **Commission** (Rimowa) and **Zak Group** (Prada, Paco Rabanne) show how luxury houses now favour plain, utilitarian sans-serif marks. That supports a neutral typographic frame for a multi-brand LP, so that dozens of maison logos do not clash.
- The large network agencies (Landor, Wolff Olins, Interbrand, FutureBrand) have few luxury fashion credits in public sources. They are lower-value references for this LP than the boutiques above.

### Inferences
Taken together with the first pass, these suggest an LP pattern:
1. **Destination voice first.** Follow Made Thought's MDD: the site speaks as one curated place with a cultural and editorial tone, not as a directory.
2. **Zoned "salons" by category.** Follow SKP-S floors, the Harrods rooms and Mulberry Paris's three sections. Each zone gets its own mood band (colour, texture, motion).
3. **Neutral typographic frame.** Follow Rimowa, Asprey and the Céline "minimal and flexible" system, so that dozens of maison logos and images coexist without clashing.
4. **Product and editorial grid.** Follow Work & Co's LOEWE and JW Anderson sites, and AKQA's earlier LOEWE "art gallery" storefront from the first pass.
5. **Wayfinding and placemaking layer.** Follow Lee Gardens and Pacific Place: a map or neighbourhood view that ties boutiques to place.
